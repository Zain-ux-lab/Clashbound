Clashbound · Battle Guide

Choose three characters, equip four moves each, and defeat the opposing team. Every move is available immediately. No progression or earned cosmetic rewards.

Current rules · reviewed 24 October 2026 · Numbers are initial playtest values.

Who goes first?

Your first selected character is your lead. You can change your lead before starting. Both teams lock their leads before the reveal.

  1. Higher lead Speed opens. Veyne gets +3 when leading.
  2. If leads tie, higher total base team Speed opens.
  3. Only an exact tie uses an equal random draw.

After the opening, turns always alternate. Speed never grants extra turns. The opening banner explains the result.

Four damage types

Magic → Elemental → Physical → Spirit → Magic. Each type is strong against the next and resisted by the previous. Same and opposite types are neutral.

Magic means runes and spellcraft; Elemental means fire, lightning and natural forces; Physical means weapons and impacts; Spirit means souls and life force.

125% means 25% extra damage; 80% means 20% less; 100% is normal. Check the move type, not the attacking character’s type. Matching your own type gives no extra bonus.

Stats and damage

Physical moves use Power against Armour. Magic, Elemental and Spirit moves use Focus against Ward. Contact is separate: a contact spell still uses Focus.

Base damage = move power × attack stat ÷ defence stat, rounded down. Then apply type, conditions, guard and passive effects. The preview shows base damage; actual HP loss can differ. Riftclaw’s pending wounds are shown separately.

HP is health; Recovery scales healing (10 = normal); Speed decides the opening only.

Passives and unique fourth moves

Passives work automatically. Your fourth move uses your turn. Read a character’s ability below or open its ability name during battle.

Mage

Runic Transcription — passive. Remember the last enemy non-Physical damaging spell you witness. Forbidden Script casts it once using your stats. Switching clears it.

Forbidden Script — fourth move. Cast your recorded enemy spell once, using your own stats. Unavailable until you witness a damaging non-Physical spell. Copies no passive.

Sporestag

Forest Memory — passive. After surviving a direct hit, take 25% less damage from that exact move until another move hits you. Switching clears the memory.

Shed the Husk — fourth move. Remove negative conditions and Forest Memory, then restore up to 8 HP scaled by Recovery. Uses your turn.

Glowmire

Last Light — passive. Survive one otherwise lethal hit at 1 HP per battle. Follow the Lantern heals a reserve and switches to it.

Follow the Lantern — fourth move. Choose a living reserve: heal it for up to 8 HP scaled by your Recovery, then switch to it. Uses your whole turn; paralysis blocks it.

Bramblebelly

Tangled Ground — passive. While active, enemy voluntary switches deal 6 damage to the departing character. Free replacements are safe.

Drag Through the Thorns — fourth move. Deal a light hit, then force the opponent to choose a living reserve for free. Their normal turn is preserved. No replacement if no reserves remain.

Veyne

Trailblazer — passive. Gain +3 opening Speed when leading. Called Shot rewards predicting the exact enemy move; it does not protect you.

Called Shot — fourth move. Predict one enemy move. After that exact move is used next enemy turn, fire a 32-power Physical shot. No protection; another move or a switch wastes the shot.

Coil

Conductive Arc — passive. Elemental attacks on the active enemy arc 20% of actual HP damage to the first living enemy reserve. Ground Fault does not arc.

Ground Fault — fourth move. Choose a living enemy reserve and strike it with 14 Elemental power. Does not hit the active enemy or trigger Conductive Arc.

Bastion

Hold the Door — passive. When you voluntarily switch out, protect the incoming ally by at least 20% against its next hit, until after the enemy turn.

Relief Guard — fourth move. Choose a living reserve and switch to it, transferring your guard (at least 20%). Never stacks; expires after the enemy turn. Paralysis blocks it.

Vesperfang

Twilight Covenant — passive. Neither active character can heal while you are active. This also blocks your own healing and drain; reserves can still be healed.

Nightfall — fourth move. Remove guard, Thorn Coat, prepared Spell Echo and Called Shot from both active characters. No damage; does not remove negative conditions.

Hushwing

Lulling Rhythm — passive. The enemy cannot repeat its previous move while you are active. Encore reverses this for one enemy turn. Always leaves a playable action.

Encore — fourth move. For the next enemy turn, require their previous available move or a switch. Unavailable until they have a usable previous move. Never removes every legal action.

Riftclaw

Out of Step — passive. Direct hits while active become pending wounds. Pay them after your next action, including switching. Switching cannot erase the wound.

Seal the Wound — fourth move. Halve your pending wound (round down), then immediately take the remaining damage. Uses your turn; unavailable with no pending damage.

Switching and targets

A voluntary switch uses your whole turn. A knockout replacement is free. Drag Through the Thorns also gives the displaced character’s owner a free choice; they still receive their normal turn. Bramblebelly’s switch damage applies only to voluntary departures.

For a targeted move, choose the reserve character or enemy move in the target picker. Cancel closes the picker without using your turn. Knocked-out targets are never selectable.

Switching clears ordinary conditions, copied spells, Forest Memory and previous-move restrictions. Riftclaw must still pay pending wounds; Glowmire’s once-per-battle Last Light does not reset.

Healing

Healing = listed amount × Recovery ÷ 10, rounded down, capped at missing HP. Drain = actual direct HP damage × Recovery ÷ 40, rounded down and capped at 4 HP. Damage delayed by Riftclaw does not immediately feed drain or Coil’s arc.

Vesperfang blocks healing of either active character, including itself and drain. Healing a reserve before switching it in remains possible.

Read the battlefield

Open a passive’s name for its explanation. Recorded spells, predictions, Encore and pending wounds appear beside the fighters. Disabled moves explain their requirements in “Moves & abilities”. The battle log names damage sources and reserve targets.

Builds contain two fixed signatures and two flexible choices. All eight move options are available immediately; there are no mastery points or earned cosmetics.
